How Fungi Reproduce

Fungi can reproduce either sexually or asexually with some species reproducing in both ways. To reproduce, fungi produce a large number of spores; there are no set genders so fungi just reproduce with whatever other fungi has the proper molecular structure to reproduce with them. To do this a fungi sends out pheromones from its mycelium which are picked up and bound to another fungi's receptors which are able to reproduce.


This bonding causes the fungi to send out their hyphae towards each other. Once the hyphae meet they fuse their cytoplasms together (this process is called plasmogamy) some time later between hours and centuries (yes, you read that right centuries) spores are produced with the fungi then dispersed. Some disperse their spores on the wind, some on the water and some fungi put their spores on passing animals to let them be deposited on a place where they will hopefully find water and nutrients, which if the spores find they grow and then when they are big enough, repeat the cycle.


Other fungi just grow a separate structure that produces spores by mitosis; these structures are called molds. In unicellular fungi like yeast reproduction occurs by cell division.



Fungi Reproduction Types
  • Asexual: Involves mitosis, producing genetically identical spores.
  • Sexual: Involves plasmogamy and karyogamy, leading to genetic recombination.
