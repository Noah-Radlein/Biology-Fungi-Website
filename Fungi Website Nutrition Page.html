How Fungi Obtain Food

Fungi are primarily heterotrophic, meaning they can't create their own food and there are currently no known species of fungi that are autotrophs. Fungi nutrition comes from 3 channels which are saprotrophism, parasitism and symbiotic relationships (mutualism).


Saprotropism is the most common means of nutrition for fungi as it is the obtaining of nutrients through the breakdown of dead organic material such as decaying wood/bark. Secondly, Parasitism is the obtaining of nutrients through the body of another organic being, such as athlete’s foot and ringworm. Lastly, through mutualism, which is the obtaining of nutrients through another organic being’s body causing no harm and benefiting both organisms such as Rumen fungi which live in herbivores' stomachs.

  • Saprophytic: Feed on dead matter (e.g., mushrooms).
  • Parasitic: Feed on living hosts (e.g., Candida).
  • Mutualistic: Form partnerships with plants (mycorrhizae).
