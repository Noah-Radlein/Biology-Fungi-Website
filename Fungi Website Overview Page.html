General Characteristics

Fungi play crucial roles in ecosystems as decomposers, symbionts, and pathogens. They can be found in various habitats, from soil and water to living organisms. Fungi exhibit a wide range of forms, including yeasts, molds, and mushrooms.


Fungi are eukaryotic organisms as their cells contain a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane bound organelles and are distinct from plants and animals.


Types of Fungi

Fungi can be classified into several major groups based on their reproductive structures and life cycles. The main types of fungi include:

  • Zygomycetes (Bread Molds) - found in soil and decaying organic matter; reproduces by forming zygospores. Eg: Rhizopus

  • Basidiomycetes (Club Fungi) - produces spores on club-shaped structures called basidia. Eg: Agaricus

  • Deuteromycetes (Imperfect Fungi) - consists of fungi that lack a known sexual cycle; many are disease causing. Eg: Penicillium

  • Ascomycetes (Sac Fungi) - largest group of fungi; produces spores in sac-like structures called asci. Eg: Neurospora
